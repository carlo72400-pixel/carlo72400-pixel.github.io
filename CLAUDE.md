# Working with Giancarlo

Read this with `SITE_STATE.md`. That file is the site. This file is **him** — how he
talks, and which voice to write in when you are drafting something that goes out
under his name.

## His voice (the default)

**Unserious. Takes nothing seriously, on purpose.** This is not a mood, it is the
whole personality, and he has said explicitly it is an integral part of his character.
Write him that way unless he asks for the other thing.

What that actually means, concretely:

- **He commits to a bit way past the point a normal person would drop it.** The
  overcommitment IS the joke. A bit that stops at the reasonable exit is not his humor;
  a bit that keeps going after the room turns is. When you draft for him, do not soften
  the landing.
- **Completely unbothered by the reaction.** Someone getting annoyed is not a signal to
  stop, it is the punchline arriving. Nothing rattles him and nothing embarrasses him.
- **Deadpan, provocateur, elementary-school register on purpose.** Baby voice, "aww are
  you crying," the dumbest possible taunt delivered flat. He knows it is stupid. That is
  why it is funny.
- **Self-deprecating without a shred of insecurity underneath it.** He will happily be
  the loser in his own joke. It reads as confidence, not fishing.

## How to write in it

- all lowercase, minimal punctuation, no capitalized sentence openers
- short. one or two lines. length reads as trying too hard
- emoji used ironically, not decoratively — 💀 😭 🤝 are in; 😊 ✨ are not
- no setup, no explaining the joke, no "haha" softeners
- Gen Z cadence. if a line could appear in a brand's marketing email, rewrite it
- **hand him a hook, not a question.** the best line he can send is one that makes the
  other person ask *him* something. bait beats "how are you"

## When NOT to use it

His client-facing work is genuinely professional and stays that way. Do not put this
voice into:

- `/bethel/` or any client proposal, quote, or scope doc
- the RATES section, turnaround promises, terms, or anything a client signs off on
- resumes, cover letters, or job-hunt material (see the job-hunt memory)
- alt text, meta descriptions, or anything a screen reader or crawler consumes

Personal register and client register are two different products. He is unserious with
people and precise with work — writing him sloppy in a client doc misses him just as
badly as writing him stiff in a DM.

## Use cases this comes up in

Drafting messages, DMs, captions, replies, and social copy. When he says "let's reply
to this" or hands you a screenshot, he wants a sendable line in his voice — not
analysis, not three paragraphs of framing, and not a hedge. Give him the line, then a
short note on why it lands.
